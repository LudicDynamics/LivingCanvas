You come back to the night library with your spell held in your palm. Inside the glass, stars that were never in this room keep turning, small enough to cover with your thumb.

Seraphina tilts it toward the candle and away again; the stars stay. She does not ask whether it is a great spell. She asks what you called it, and repeats the name back slowly, the way you read a label on a jar.

“It doesn’t open anything,” she says. “It keeps something. That is a different kind of brave.”

The librarian notes it in her ledger — one small mirror, out on loan, contents: a sky. Whenever a night feels too closed, you can open your hand and look.

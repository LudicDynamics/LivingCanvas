You say it plainly: you are going, and you would rather not go alone. Iris looks at the door for a long moment, then at you, and takes her coat from the hook as if she has been waiting a long time for someone to ask.

Outside is the place the door wrote for you, the one you have already walked once. This time neither of you turns back toward the cabin. Behind you the lamp goes out on its own, and the rain stops before you reach the bend.

Somewhere along the track she takes her own envelope from her pocket, turns it over once, and puts it back unopened. "Later," she says. "When we're somewhere that was already there before we opened the door."

You still don't know how you arrived. But this time someone else will remember the room, and you will remember her.

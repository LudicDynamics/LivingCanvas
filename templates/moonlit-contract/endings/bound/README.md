You say it plainly: stay. Not until the door is safe, not until the night is over. Stay.

Lyra is quiet for a long moment. Then she kneels in the faded circle and holds out her wrist, and you tie the blue ribbon around it and around your own, in one knot.

The last of the lavender light sinks into the stone. You both wait for her edges to flicker the way they have all night. They do not.

When the sun comes up over the sea she is still there, squinting at it as if she had forgotten what morning looks like. “Then I will walk with you,” she says, “for as long as you keep asking.”

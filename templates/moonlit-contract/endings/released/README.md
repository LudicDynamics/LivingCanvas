You untie the ribbon from her gauntlet yourself. The night no longer needs her sword, you tell her. She can rest.

Lyra looks at the loose ribbon in your hand for a long time. Then she lays her sword on the observatory floor, flat, the way one sets down something finished.

“One pact in my life, kept by both sides,” she says. “I had hoped for that much.” The lavender light rises around her like slow sparks, and she lifts one hand to you as it takes her.

The wind carries the ribbon out over the sea. The sword stays on the stone until morning, and no one comes to claim it.
